Software development firms continually face challenges in preparing accurate technical proposals, estimating development costs, and predicting project timelines. These processes are typically manual, heavily dependent on individual experience, and prone to inconsistencies that negatively affect pricing accuracy, proposal quality, and overall project planning. Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited, a technology company dedicated to building client and stakeholder ideas into reality through technology innovation, is one such firm that experiences these operational inefficiencies. Despite advancements in project management tools and estimation of software, most existing solutions remain generic and fail to address the specific complexities inherent in software development engagements. Current tools largely rely on manual input, thereby lacking the intelligence required to analyze technical requirements, suggest optimal architectures, or generate context-aware cost breakdowns.
This research presents the design and implementation of an AI-Powered Technical Proposal and Cost Estimation System specifically tailored for software development firms. The system automates the requirement analysis process, generates structured technical proposals, recommends appropriate technology stacks and system architectures, and provides data-driven cost and timeline estimations based on historical project data and predefined parameters.
The system was developed following an Incremental Software Development Model and incorporates Large Language Model (LLM) assisted analysis, generation, and estimation — accessed through the NVIDIA NIM API (model meta/llama-3.3-70b-instruct) with deterministic local fallbacks — together with a structured proposal generation engine, a project repository for historical reference, and a role-based user management module.

GENERAL INTRODUCTION
Introduction
The global software development industry is characterized by rapid growth, increasing client demands, and intense competition among firms striving to deliver quality solutions within tight timelines and budgets. At the heart of every successful software project lies an accurate and well-structured technical proposal accompanied by a realistic cost and timeline estimation. These foundational documents serve as the contractual and operational basis upon which client relationships and project outcomes are built.
Despite their critical importance, the process of preparing technical proposals and estimating project costs in most software development firms remains largely manual, experience-driven, and inconsistent. Software engineers and project managers often rely on personal judgment and informal historical references to scope projects and determine pricing, a practice that introduces significant variability and risk. Miscalculations in cost or timeline estimates can result in financial losses, strained client relationships, and project failures that damage the firm's reputation.
Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited, a technology company registered in Nigeria and dedicated to turning client and stakeholder ideas into reality through technology innovation, faces these exact challenges. As the company grows and takes on increasingly diverse and complex projects, the need for a systematic, intelligent, and automated approach to proposal generation and cost estimation has become apparent. The absence of such a system not only limits the firm's operational efficiency but also poses risks to the consistency and professionalism of its client engagements. This research is therefore motivated by the practical need to address these inefficiencies within Jimstech and, by extension, similar software development firms facing comparable challenges.
Artificial intelligence offers transformation potential in addressing these operational inefficiencies. Through natural language processing, machine learning-based estimation models, and intelligent recommendation engines, AI systems can analyze project requirements, suggest appropriate technology stacks, generate structured proposal documents, and produce data-driven cost and timeline projections with far greater consistency and accuracy than manual processes allow.
Background of the Study
Software development firms across Africa and globally are under increasing pressure to deliver precise project proposals and accurate cost estimates to win client confidence and maintain profitability. The challenge is particularly acute for growing firms like Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited, which operates in the competitive Nigerian technology ecosystem where speed, precision, and professionalism in client engagement are critical differentiation.
Currently, the proposal and estimation process at most software development firms, including Jimstech Innovations, relies heavily on manual methods. A project manager or senior engineer typically reviews a client brief, draws experience, consults team members informally, and assembles a proposal document using generic templates. Cost estimates are derived from rough calculations based on team rates and guessed timelines, often without systematic reference to historical project data or structured estimation methodologies.
This approach leads to a number of well-documented problems in the software engineering literature. According to the Standish Group's CHAOS Report (2020), a significant proportion of software projects exceed their original budget and timeline estimates, with poor initial estimation being a primary contributing factor. Research by Jorgensen and Shepperd (2007) further established that expert-based estimation, while widely practiced, is subject to cognitive biases and anchoring effects that reduce accuracy over time.
The emergence of artificial intelligence and machine learning has opened new avenues for addressing these estimation challenges. Studies by Azzeh et al. (2015) and Nassif et al. (2019) have demonstrated that AI-based models can achieve significantly higher estimation accuracy than traditional parametric and expert-based methods when prompted on sufficient historical project data.
Furthermore, large language models and natural language processing technologies now make it possible to automatically analyze client requirement documents and generate structured proposal content. Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited was incorporated under the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020 in Nigeria with a mandate to carry on the business of software development and digital solutions, educational technology, artificial intelligence and data analytic s, and e-commerce and online marketplaces. As the company expands its client base and project portfolio, the absence of a systematic proposal and estimation system represents a significant operational gap that this research seeks to address.
Statement of the Problem
Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited, like many software development firms operating in the Nigerian technology sector, encounters persistent operational challenges in the areas of technical proposal preparation and project cost estimation. These challenges have direct consequences for the firm's efficiency, profitability, client satisfaction, and competitive positioning. The core problems identified include:
Absence of a centralized digital system for capturing, analyzing, and processing client project requirements in a structured manner, resulting in incomplete or inconsistent requirement of documentation that leads to inaccurate proposal scoping.
Heavy reliance on manual and experience-based methods for generating technical proposals, which consumes significant time and produces inconsistent documentation quality across different team members and project types.
Lack of a data-driven cost and timeline estimation framework, causing frequent budget overruns and missed deadlines that undermine client trust and project profitability.
Absence of a systematic technology stack recommendation mechanism, leading to ad hoc technology decisions that may not always align with project requirements or organizational capabilities.
No centralized project repository to store and retrieve historical project data, proposals, cost records, and outcomes for reference and continuous improvement of estimation accuracy.
Inadequate role-based access controls and user management for the proposal and estimation workflow, resulting in lack of accountability and potential for unauthorized modifications to critical project documents. In the absence of clearly defined access permissions, sensitive proposal information and financial estimates may be viewed or altered by individuals without the appropriate authorization, raising concerns about data integrity, confidentiality, and governance.
These problems collectively result in slow and inefficient clients on boarding, increased risk of financial losses, and reduced capacity for the organization to scale its operations effectively. There is therefore a clear and urgent need for an intelligent, automated system that can streamline these processes and deliver measurable improvements in proposal quality and estimation accuracy.

This project focuses on the design and implementation of an AI-Powered Technical Proposal and Cost Estimation System specifically tailored to the operational needs of Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited. Description of the Current System
The current system for managing technical proposal preparation and project cost estimation at Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited is primarily manual, template-driven, and heavily reliant on individual expertise and informal communication. This traditional approach covers three core operational areas: requirement gathering, proposal documentation, and cost and timeline estimation.
Requirement Gathering Process
Manual Client Brief Collection: When a potential client approaches Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited with a project idea, the initial requirement gathering is conducted through unstructured conversations, email exchanges, or informal meetings. There is no standardized
digital form or system for capturing client requirements, resulting in incomplete and inconsistently documented project briefs.
No Structured Requirement Classification: Captured requirements are not systematically classified by type, complexity, or priority. Project managers and engineers rely entirely on personal judgment to assess the scope and complexity of a project, leading to variability in scope assessments across different team members.
Proposal Documentation Process
Template-Based Manual Drafting: Technical proposals are prepared by manually filling in generic Microsoft Word or Google Docs templates. Each proposal requires the project Manager or lead engineer to write from scratch or adapt to a previous proposal, a process that is time-consuming, inconsistent, and dependent on the quality of the individual author.
Inconsistent Proposal Quality: Because there is no standardized system for proposal generation, the quality and structure of proposals vary significantly depending on who prepares them. Some proposals may include detailed system architecture descriptions while others omit them entirely; some may contain thorough technology stack justifications while others provide only superficial technology mentions.
No Automated Content Generation: The current process has no capability for automatically generating proposal sections based on analyzed requirements. All content must be written manually, even for sections that follow predictable patterns such as project overview, scope of work, deliverable, and technology recommendations.
Cost and Timeline Estimation Process
Experience-Based Informal Estimation: Cost and timeline estimates are derived primarily from the personal experience of senior engineers and project managers. There is no formal estimation model or structured methodology in use. Estimates are often communicated verbally before being formalized in the proposal document, introducing the risk of inconsistency between the verbal commitment and the written document.
No Historical Data Reference System: Jimstech Innovations currently has no centralized repository of historical project data that could be used to reference past cost and timeline performance. When estimating a new project, team members may attempt to recall similar past projects from memory, but without structured data access, this recall is imprecise and unreliable.
No Technology Recommendation Logic: The selection of technology stacks for new projects is done informally, typically defaulting to the technologies most familiar to the available development team rather than systematically evaluating which technologies best fit the project's requirements, scalability needs, and long-term maintainability. This ad hoc approach to technology selection can result in mismatches between the chosen tools and the demands of the project, potentially increasing development effort, limiting system performance, and creating technical debt that becomes costly to address in later stages. The lack of a structured recommendation process also means the firm may overlook emerging technologies.
Analysis of the Current System
The current manual system for managing technical proposal preparation and cost estimation at Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited faces several operational challenges that limit its effectiveness. Below are the key weaknesses identified during the analysis:
Lack of Structured Requirement Capture
No centralized digital system exists for capturing, storing, and retrieving client project requirements in a structured format.
Requirements are scattered across emails, chat messages, and informal notes, making it difficult to maintain a complete and accurate record of what was agreed with the client.
Inconsistent Proposal Quality

Problems of the Current System
The current manual proposal process at Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited is slow and highly dependent on individual staff experience. Response Time: The time from receiving a client brief to delivering a completed technical proposal typically ranges from several days to over a week in the current manual system. Incomplete Information
Input: The absence of a structured intake form or requirement analysis tool means that the information collected from clients is often incomplete, ambiguous, or inconsistently formatted. Output: The proposals produced by the current system vary significantly in quality, structure, and comprehensiveness. System Architectural Design
System architecture design is a critical phase in the development of the AI-Powered Technical Proposal and Cost Estimation System, as it defines the structural framework and outlines how the different components of the system interact to fulfill both functional and non-functional requirements. It involves planning the major modules, system interfaces, user access levels, AI processing pipelines, and the deployment environment to ensure the platform is scalable, secure, and maintainable.
The architecture consists of well-defined layers that separate responsibilities and promote modularity:
Presentation Layer: The single-page application built with React 18 and TypeScript, bundled by Vite, styled with Tailwind CSS v4, and composed of Radix UI and MUI components. Role-specific views are rendered for Software Engineers, Project Managers, Sales, and System Administrators.
Application Logic Layer: The business logic runs in the browser (React hooks/services) and coordinates requirement submission, proposal generation, cost estimation, timeline prediction, and technology recommendation; role-based access is enforced in the UI.
AI Assisted Logic Layer: calls the NVIDIA LLM API for analysis and generation, with deterministic local fallbacks to determine the requirement analysis engine, proposal generation module, and technology recommendation engine. This layer interfaces with the project repository to prompt historical data for model inference.
Data and Authentication Layer: Supabase provides managed PostgreSQL, authentication (email/password with JWT sessions), and Row-Level Security (RLS) policies. The SPA talks to the database through Supabase PostgREST; database triggers maintain the audit log and notifications.
API and Communication Layer: Facilitates communication between the frontend interface and backend services using RESTful APIs, enabling modular and scalable integration of all system components and supporting future integration with external systems.
Key architectural considerations include scalability to accommodate growing data volumes and user loads, security through role-based access control and encrypted communications, maintainability through modular design enabling independent component updates, and reliability through appropriate error handling and data backup mechanisms.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Conclusion
The development of the AI-Powered Technical Proposal and Cost Estimation System for Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited represents a significant step forward in addressing the operational inefficiencies that characterize manual proposal preparation and cost estimation processes in software development firms. This project was initiated in direct response to the persistent challenges faced by Jimstech Innovations in managing the pre-project client engagement lifecycle, particularly in the areas of structured requirement analysis, consistent technical proposal generation, and accurate cost and timeline estimation.
The manual systems previously in use at Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited were characterized by heavy reliance on individual expertise, absence of standardized processes, lack of systematic historical data utilization, and significant consumption of senior technical staff time on administrative documentation tasks. These inefficiencies resulted in inconsistent proposal quality, frequent estimation inaccuracies, extended client response times, and reduced capacity for the organization to scale its business development operations effectively as it grew.
In conclusion, the AI-Powered Technical Proposal and Cost Estimation System stands as a practical and impactful contribution to both the academic body of knowledge at the Adventist University of Central Africa and the operational capabilities of Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited. With proper deployment, user training, data migration, and ongoing maintenance, this system has the measurable potential to improve the firm's efficiency, profitability, and competitive positioning in the Nigerian technology.
Furthermore, the successful implementation of this system will demonstrate the effectiveness of integrating artificial intelligence into core business processes, paving the way for future enhancements and wider adoption of intelligent decision-support systems within the organization.
Recommendations
To ensure effective deployment and maximum benefit from the AI-Powered Technical Proposal and Cost Estimation System, the following recommendations are offered to the management and staff of Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited:
System Deployment and Structured User Training: The system should be formally deployed across all relevant teams at Jimstech Innovations Nigeria Limited, with structured training sessions conducted for all user roles including System Administrators, Project Managers, Software Engineers, and Client-facing staff. Training should cover both the technical operation of the system and the organizational workflow changes that accompany its adoption.
Historical Data Migration: To maximize the effectiveness of the AI estimation models from the outset, the organization should invest time in migrating historical project data including past proposals, cost records, actual timeline data, and project outcomes into the system's project repository. The quality and quantity of historical data directly determine the accuracy of AI-generated estimates.
Continuous Model Refinement: Because the system uses a hosted Large Language Model, continuous improvement is achieved through prompt and context refinement rather than retraining. As the project repository accumulates completed projects, that historical data should be curated and supplied to the model as richer prompt context, and the prompts/parameters periodically reviewed and validated against actual project outcomes to improve estimate accuracy. The deterministic local fallbacks should also be tuned as more real cost/timeline data becomes available.
Integration with Client Management Systems: Future development should explore integration between this system and any client relationship management tools or project management platforms used by Jimstech Innovations, enabling seamless data flow from initial client contact through proposal delivery and project execution tracking.
Regular Performance Monitoring and Evaluation: Key performance indicators such as proposal preparation time reduction, estimation accuracy rate, client proposal acceptance rate, and user satisfaction scores should be tracked regularly to quantify the system's operational impact and identify areas for further improvement.

Description | This use case enables the system to predict a realistic project timeline based on project complexity, team size, required technologies, and historical delivery data.
Pre-condition | Project requirements must have been analyzed. Historical project timeline data must be available in the system.
Post-condition | A predicted timeline with milestones and delivery phases is generated and associated with the project proposal.
Normal Flow | 1. System retrieves analyzed project complexity and resource requirements. 2. AI engine applies timeline prediction models using historical data and current project parameters. 3. System generates a phased timeline with key milestones (design, development, testing, deployment). 4. Predicted timeline is attached to the proposal. 5. Software Engineer reviews and adjusts the timeline if necessary.
Alternative Flow | If the system lacks sufficient data for a confident prediction, it generates a conservative estimate and recommends manual review by the Software Engineer.
Name | Recommend Project Technologies
Actor | AI Engine (automated), Software Engineer
Description | This use case allows the system to suggest appropriate technologies, frameworks, and tools based on the submitted project requirements and industry best practices.
Pre-condition | Project requirements must have been analyzed. A technology knowledge base must be configured and up to date in the system.
Post-condition | A list of recommended technologies with justifications is generated and linked to project analysis. The Software Engineer can review, accept, or modify the recommendations.
Normal Flow | 1. System retrieves analyzed project type and technical requirements. 2. AI engine queries the technology knowledge base. 3. System matches project needs suitable technology stacks. 4. System generates a ranked list of technology recommendations with justifications. 5. Software Engineer reviews and approves or modifies recommendations. 6. Approved recommendations are included in the project proposal.
Alternative Flow | If no strong match is found, the system provides general recommendations based on project category and flags the result for manual expert input.
Name | Manage Project Repository
Actor | System Administrator, Project Manager, Software Engineer (read only)
Description | This use case allows authorized users to create, organize, retrieve, update, and archive project records including proposals, cost estimates, timelines, and supporting documents.
Pre-condition | The user must be logged in with appropriate permissions. At least one project record must exist in the system (for retrieval/update operations).
Post-condition | Project records are created, updated, or archived as requested. Changes are logged in the system audit trail.
Normal Flow | 1. User navigates to the project repository section. 2. User selects an action (create, view, edit, or archive a project). 3. System retrieves or accepts the relevant project data. 4. User completes the required action (e.g., updates project status). 5. System saves the changes and confirms the action to the user.
Alternative Flow | If the user attempts to access or modify a record without sufficient permission, the system denies the action and displays an authorization error.
Name | Generate Reports and Analytics
Actor | System Administrator, Sales, Project Manager
Description | This use case allows authorized users to generate and view reports on system activity, proposal outcomes, cost estimation accuracy, and other performance analytics.
Pre-condition | The user must be logged in with report generation privileges. Relevant data must be available in the system.
Post-condition | The requested report is generated and displayed. The report can be exported in supported formats (e.g., PDF, Excel).
Normal Flow | 1. User navigates to the Reports & Analytics section. 2. User selects the report type and specifies parameters (date range, project type, etc.). 3. System retrieves and processes the relevant data. 4. System generates and displays the report with charts or tables as appropriate. 5. User reviews the report and optionally exports it.
Alternative Flow | If no data matches the selected parameters, the system notifies the user that no records are available for the specified criteria.
Name | Manage Templates and Integrations
Actor | System Administrator, Project Manager
Description | This use case allows the System Administrator to create, update, configure, or remove proposal templates and manage third-party tool integrations used by the system.
Pre-condition | The user must be logged in as a System Administrator or Project Manager. The templates and integrations management module must be accessible.
Post-condition | Templates are created, updated, or removed as requested. Integration settings are saved and applied to the system.
Normal Flow | 1. Administrator navigates to the Templates and Integrations settings. 2. Administrator selects an action (add, edit, or remove a template). 3. System displays the relevant configuration form. 4.Administrator provides the required information and saves the changes. 5. System validates and applies to the new configuration. 6. System confirms the successful update to the administrator.
Alternative Flow | If a template is in use by active projects, the system warns the administrator before allowing deletion or modification.
Name | Perform Security Audit
Actor | System Administrator
Description | This use case allows the System Administrator to review system access logs, identify security anomalies, and ensure that all user activities comply with system security policies.
Pre-condition | The administrator must be logged in with security audit privileges. System audit logs must be enabled and populated.
Post-condition | An audit report is generated detailing user activities, access events, and any flagged anomalies. Suspicious activities are reported for further investigation.
Normal Flow | 1. Administrator navigates to the Security Audit section. 2. Administrator selects the audit scope and time period. 3. System retrieves and analyzes the relevant logs. 4. System generates an audit report highlighting key events and flagged anomalies. 5. Administrator reviews the report and takes appropriate action on any identified issues.
Alternative Flow | If the audit logs are incomplete or corrupted, the system alerts the administrator and recommends a system integrity check.
Name | Manage User Accounts
Actor | System Administrator
Description | This use case allows the System Administrator to create, update, deactivate, or delete user accounts, and to assign or modify user roles and permissions within the system.
Pre-condition | The administrator must be logged in with user management privileges.
Post-condition | User accounts are created, updated, deactivated, or deleted as requested. Role assignments are applied, and changes are logged in the system.
Normal Flow | 1. Administrator navigates to the User Management section. 2. Administrator selects an action (add, edit, deactivate, or remove a user). 3. System displays the relevant user management form. 4. Administrator provides or modifies the necessary information (name, email, role, status). 5. System validates and applies to the changes. 6. System confirms the update and notifies the affected user if applicable.
Alternative Flow | If the administrator attempts to delete an account associated with active projects, the system warns them and recommends deactivation instead of permanent deletion.
