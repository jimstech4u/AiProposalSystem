# Key Issues and Analysis of the Current System

## 1. Key Issues with the Current System

1. **Manual Requirement Collection**  
   The current system depends on informal discussions, emails, calls, and scattered notes to collect client requirements. This often leads to incomplete, unclear, or inconsistent requirement documentation.

2. **Slow Proposal Preparation**  
   Technical proposals are prepared manually using generic templates and personal experience. This consumes significant time and delays the response to potential clients.

3. **Inaccurate Cost Estimation**  
   Project costs are estimated mainly through human judgment instead of structured data or historical project records. This increases the risk of underestimating or overestimating project budgets.

4. **Poor Timeline Prediction**  
   Project timelines are often predicted without a systematic analysis of project complexity, team capacity, dependencies, and previous project performance. This can result in unrealistic delivery schedules.

5. **Lack of Centralized Historical Records**  
   Previous proposals, project outcomes, cost estimates, timelines, and lessons learned are not stored in a centralized repository. As a result, the organization cannot easily reuse past knowledge to improve future proposals.

## 2. Analysis of the Current System Based on Key Issues

1. **Inefficient Requirement Handling**  
   Because requirements are collected manually, important project details may be missed during the early scoping stage. This affects proposal accuracy and may lead to changes after the proposal has already been submitted.

2. **High Dependence on Individual Experience**  
   The quality of proposals and estimates depends heavily on the knowledge and availability of specific staff members. If an experienced manager or engineer is unavailable, proposal quality may reduce.

3. **Inconsistent Proposal Quality**  
   Since proposals are manually prepared, different staff members may use different formats, writing styles, technical explanations, and levels of detail. This reduces professionalism and standardization.

4. **Limited Data-Driven Decision Making**  
   The current system does not use structured historical data to support cost, timeline, or technology decisions. This makes the estimation process subjective and less reliable.

5. **Difficulty Tracking Proposal Progress**  
   There is no centralized dashboard for monitoring proposal status, pending estimations, approvals, or client feedback. This makes it harder for managers to track work progress and responsibilities.

6. **Weak Accountability and Access Control**  
   Manual files and shared documents make it difficult to identify who created, modified, reviewed, or approved a proposal. This creates accountability and document control challenges.

7. **Reduced Operational Efficiency**  
   Repeated manual work, duplicated calculations, and lack of automation slow down the proposal workflow. This reduces the company’s ability to respond quickly to clients and compete effectively.
